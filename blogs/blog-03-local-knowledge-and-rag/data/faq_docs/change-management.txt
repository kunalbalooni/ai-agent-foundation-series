Change Management Policy

Definition
A change is any modification to production systems including code deployments, infrastructure updates, database schema changes, configuration changes, and third-party integration updates. All changes must be logged, categorised, and approved before deployment.

Change categories
- Standard change: Pre-approved, low-risk, follows a documented runbook. No additional approval required.
- Normal change: Requires review and approval via the change advisory board (CAB) process. Minimum 48-hour lead time.
- Emergency change: Deployed outside the normal process due to critical business or security need. Must be retroactively logged and reviewed within 24 hours.

Approval workflow
1. Engineer raises a change request in the change management system with a description, impact assessment, rollback plan, and test evidence.
2. The change is reviewed by the team lead.
3. Normal changes are reviewed by the CAB at the weekly change review meeting.
4. Approved changes are scheduled in the release calendar.
5. Deployment is confirmed in #deployments with a link to the change record.

Rollback and validation
- Every change must include a rollback procedure tested in a staging environment.
- Post-deployment validation checks must be defined before approval.
- If validation fails after deployment, the rollback procedure is executed immediately.

Emergency changes
- Emergency changes bypass the CAB process but require real-time approval from the on-call engineering manager.
- All emergency changes must be documented within 24 hours using the emergency change template.
- Repeated emergency changes for the same system trigger a mandatory reliability review.

FAQ
- Q: What counts as a configuration change?
  A: Any modification to environment variables, feature flags, infrastructure parameters, or third-party service settings that affects production behaviour.
- Q: Can a standard change be promoted to a normal change?
  A: Yes, if the scope or risk increases beyond what the standard runbook covers, re-categorise and follow the normal change process.
- Q: Who chairs the CAB?
  A: The Release Manager chairs the CAB. Engineering leads, security, and operations are standing members.
- Q: How long are change records retained?
  A: Minimum 12 months, or longer if required by applicable compliance frameworks.
